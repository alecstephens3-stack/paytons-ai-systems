- **Decision and date:** [decision]
- **Expected result and honest odds:** [expectation]
- **Actual result:** [evidence]
- **Baseline and target comparison:** [same source, population and measurement window, or explain the change]
- **Leading indicator and guardrail:** [what moved, what did not, and any deterioration]
- **Data limitations:** [missing observations, small sample, delayed effects or competing causes]
- **Diagnosis:** [execution failure, wrong hypothesis, noise, changed conditions, poor metric, or unresolved]
- **Evidence that changed the map:** [what became more or less likely]
- **Updated probability range:** [new range and why]
- **Controlling assumption:** [what proved true or false]
- **Kill criterion reached:** [yes, no, or unclear]
- **Process quality:** [what the decision process did well or missed]
- **Outcome luck:** [what was outside control]
- **Next decision or action:** [one action]
- **Continue / change / stop:** [choice tied to the original conditions; explain any revised criterion]
- **Next review:** [owner and date or observable trigger, or none if the decision is closed]

Do not rewrite the original record. Link this review to it.

A good process can lose once, and a bad process can win once. Judge the process and outcome separately, then use the outcome to improve the next forecast.

If the result is learning, name the uncertainty resolved, the new evidence, and the decision it changes. Keep the experiment's cost visible. Saved instructions and a completed review establish process use; sustained outcome evidence is needed before claiming improvement.
